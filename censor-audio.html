Bleep out swear words in audio and video.

Drop a podcast, a stream clip, a song or a video. AI transcribes it, marks every swear word, and replaces each one with a bleep or silence. Click any other word, such as a name or an address, to bleep that too. The length never changes, so a video stays in sync. It all runs in your browser; nothing is uploaded.

How it finds the words

The recording is transcribed in your browser by Whisper, the open speech model, in a version that also gives the time of every word. Whisper writes profanity out in full rather than starring it, so each word of the transcript is checked against a list: the strong words and everything built on them, the common slurs, and, if you tick the box, mild words like damn and hell. The list matches whole words where it has to. "Hell" does not catch "hello", "ass" does not catch "class" or "assume", and "Scunthorpe" is left alone. Words with an everyday meaning, such as bloody or screw, are not on the list at all, because a DIY video or a sermon would come out bleeped throughout; add them under Extra words if you need them.

You are not limited to swearing. Every word in the transcript is a button. Click a name, a street, a company or a phone number and it is bleeped the same way. Click a marked word to let it through, for example a quoted title. Shift-click any word to hear it in context before you decide.

Why the bleep lands on the word

Word timestamps are a guide, not the cut. Whisper's start times are close, but its end times run early, so a bleep placed exactly on them would let the last syllable through: "fu-" bleeped and "-cking" left audible, which is worse than no bleep at all. The page measures the recording's own background level and extends each bleep to where the word's sound actually stops, without running into the next word. It errs on the wide side, because clipping the edge of the next word is a smaller mistake than leaving part of the swear audible. In our test recordings, with swear words laid into running speech at known times, every one was found, nothing else was marked, and the loudest part of a word left outside its bleep was 28 dB down, which you cannot hear under the tone. The cost is that a bleep can take in up to about a quarter of a second of the following word. Shift-click to check any you are unsure of.

The bleep is a 1 kHz tone, the broadcast standard, set to the level of the word it replaces rather than a fixed loudness, so it does not jump out at the listener. Silence is the other option, the usual choice for YouTube and podcasts that do not want the tone. Both fade in and out over 5 ms, so there is no click at either end.

Video stays in sync

Nothing is cut out; each word is replaced in place, so the clean audio is exactly as long as the original. For a video, the picture is copied as it is, with no re-encoding and no quality loss, and the new sound is written beside it as AAC. The lips move on the same frames they always did. That makes this the quick route for a clip with one or two slips going to YouTube, TikTok, Instagram or a school or work channel.

Where it falls short

  • Mumbled and shouted words. If Whisper does not hear a word clearly it may write something else, and a word it does not write cannot be matched. Listen to the result before you publish, and click any word it misheard.
  • Songs. Singing over loud music is harder to transcribe than speech. Separating the vocal first with the stem splitter helps, and you can then bleep the vocal stem and mix it back.
  • Other languages. Whisper transcribes many languages, but the built-in list is English. For another language, type the words under Extra words exactly as Whisper writes them.
  • Overlapping speakers. When two people talk at once, the bleep covers both of them for the length of the word.

FAQ

How do I bleep out swear words in a video?

Drop the video, press Find the swear words, check the highlighted words in the transcript, and press Make the clean version. You get the same video with each word bleeped or muted, in sync, with the picture untouched.

Can I bleep a name or other word that is not a swear word?

Yes. Every word in the transcript can be clicked; click a name, place or number and it is bleeped the same way. You can also type words under Extra words.

Can I use silence instead of a bleep?

Yes. Set Replace with to Silence. Both options fade in and out over 5 ms so there is no click.

Does it change the length of my video?

No. Each word is replaced in place, so the audio is exactly the same length and the picture is copied without re-encoding.

Is my file uploaded?

No. The speech model runs in your browser. It downloads once, about 135 MB, and is then kept by the browser.

Does it catch every swear word?

It catches every listed word that Whisper writes down. A word that is mumbled, shouted or sung over loud music can be misheard, so listen to the result and click anything it missed.
