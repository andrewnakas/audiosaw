How it works

An EPUB is a zip of web pages plus a list, the spine, that gives their reading order. The page reads that list, turns each document into plain paragraphs, drops the cover, copyright and contents pages, and names each chapter from its first heading. A text file is split wherever a line looks like a heading: "Chapter 12", "CHAPTER XII", "Part Two", "Prologue", or a Markdown # or ##. A long text with no headings at all is cut into parts of about six thousand words at paragraph breaks. You can untick chapters (an index, the acknowledgements) and rename any of them before you start.

Each chapter is then read sentence by sentence by Kokoro, an open neural text-to-speech model, on your GPU if the browser can use it and on the CPU otherwise. Numbers, prices, years and abbreviations are written out first, so "1847" is read as a year and "Dr." as "Doctor". As soon as a chapter is finished it is compressed — AAC for the M4B, MP3 for the zip — and saved in the browser. A long book never has to sit in memory as raw audio, and an interrupted run carries on from the last finished chapter instead of from page one.

At the end, the chapters are joined without re-encoding and the M4B gets a chapter track with your titles, plus the book title and author as tags. Apple Books, VLC, Smart AudioBook Player on Android, BookPlayer on iOS and most dedicated audiobook apps show those chapters and remember your place.

How long it takes

Speech runs at roughly 150 words a minute, so a 90,000-word novel is about ten hours of audio. On a laptop with a GPU that the browser can use (current Chrome or Edge on a desktop), making it takes about half as long as listening to it: a ten-hour novel in about five. On the CPU it takes several times as long. Either way a short story or an article is done in minutes, and a full novel is an evening's or an overnight job. The estimate under the chapter list is for your book and your machine. Keep the tab open; a laptop should be plugged in, because this works its processor hard. Because finished chapters are saved, a full book can be made over several sessions.

Choosing the voice

For long listening, the voices graded A and B in the list hold up best: Heart and Bella (American) and Emma (British) have the most natural rhythm over hours. Try "hear the opening" with two or three before committing to a whole book. 1.0× is the model's natural pace; most audiobook listeners speed up in their player, which keeps the pitch steady anyway, so there is little reason to bake a faster speed into the file. Every chapter is labelled in its tags as synthetic speech.

What it can't do

It reads, it doesn't act: one voice for the whole book, in a calm narrator's style, with no character voices and no shouting or whispering. Pronunciation of invented names is espeak-ng's best guess, which is usually reasonable and occasionally odd; respell a name in the text to fix it. Footnotes are skipped, and tables, captions and images are not described. EPUBs bought from Kindle, Apple Books or Kobo are usually DRM-protected and cannot be read by anything outside the store's app; the page says so if you drop one. Project Gutenberg, Standard Ebooks and most independent authors' EPUBs are DRM-free. Only English voices are available for now.

Respect copyright: making an audiobook of a book you own for your own listening is one thing; distributing it is another. Public-domain books are free for anything.

FAQ

Is it free, with no word limit?

Yes. The voice model runs on your own computer, so there is nothing per word or per hour to pay for. The only limits are your patience and the browser's storage, and a full novel fits comfortably.

Will the M4B show chapters on my phone?

Yes, in Apple Books, BookPlayer, Smart AudioBook Player, VLC and most audiobook apps. The chapter track holds the titles shown in the list, which you can edit before you start.

Can I stop and carry on later?

Yes. Every finished chapter is saved in the browser. Press Make audiobook again with the same book, voice and speed, and the saved chapters are reused; only the unfinished ones are read.

Why can't it open my Kindle or Apple Books purchase?

Those files are DRM-protected: encrypted so only the store's app can display them. No browser tool can read them. DRM-free EPUBs, plain text and pasted text all work.

Is my book uploaded?

No. The book is read in this tab and never sent anywhere; the only downloads are the voice model and the audio encoder, which are the same for everyone.

Which languages does it read?

English (American or British voices), Spanish, French, Italian, Brazilian Portuguese and Hindi. Pick a voice in the book's language; number and abbreviation expansion is English-only, so write numbers out in words in other languages for the best result.
